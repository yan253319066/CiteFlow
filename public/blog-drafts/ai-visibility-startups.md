# AI Visibility for Startups with No Backlinks

**Phase 3, Article 13**

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Backlinks are irrelevant to LLM citations.** Unlike Google's PageRank, the RAG pipeline (Article 2) does not factor link authority. A zero-backlink domain with entity clarity can outperform a high-DR domain with weak entity signals.
- **Entity establishment is the startup substitute for domain authority.** Brands that define their entity clearly, consistently, and across multiple sources (Wikipedia, Wikidata, Crunchbase) are cited at the same rate as established brands in the same category.
- **Comparison pages are the fastest path to citations for new brands.** A "Startup vs. Incumbent" comparison page creates immediate entity relationships that the model can use.
- **Product Hunt and directory submissions build the entity graph.** Each directory listing creates a structured citation the model can resolve against.
- **First-mover advantage is real in GEO.** Early entity establishment in a new category positions a startup to capture the lion's share of AI citations before competitors establish theirs.

---

For startups, the most common objection to investing in AI visibility is "We have no backlinks, so we cannot compete." This objection is based on an SEO mindset that does not apply to generative engine optimization.

## Why Backlinks Do Not Matter for AI

The RAG pipeline does not use link authority signals. The four stages — query analysis, vector retrieval, re-ranking, and citation synthesis (Article 2) — evaluate content based on semantic relevance and information gain. A page from a zero-backlink domain with clear entity definition, structured data, and unique content can rank higher in the retrieval set than a page from a high-DR domain with generic content.

This is the fundamental difference of GEO (Article 4). Traditional SEO requires building authority through links. GEO requires building authority through entity clarity. Startups can achieve the latter without the years of link building that SEO demands.

## Entity Establishment for Startups

**Wikipedia.** A Wikipedia page is the single highest-impact entity signal for a startup. It is the source from which all LLMs derive entity information. Startups should prioritize Wikipedia eligibility — notability through media coverage, funding announcements, or industry recognition.

**Wikidata.** Even without a Wikipedia page, a Wikidata entry (QID) provides structured entity data that models can resolve. Create a Wikidata entry for the startup with name, description, category, founding date, and website.

**Crunchbase, G2, Capterra.** Each directory listing creates a structured citation. These listings are indexed by LLMs as entity references. The aggregation of directory signals builds entity confidence.

**Self-published entity definition.** On your own domain, apply Article 9's Layer 1 (Entity Definition) on every page. Organization schema with @id is essential.

## Comparison Pages as Growth Lever

For a startup, a "Startup vs. Incumbent" comparison page (Article 8) is the fastest path to AI citations. The page creates an entity relationship between a known brand (the incumbent) and an unknown brand (the startup). The model that retrieves the comparison page learns the relationship and can cite the startup in future queries about the category.

## Directory Submissions for Entity Graph Building

Every directory submission — Product Hunt, G2, Capterra, AlternativeTo, SaaSHub, BetaList — creates a structured citation that models can resolve. Unlike backlinks, which transfer authority, directory listings transfer entity recognition. Each listing reinforces the startup's entity definition.
